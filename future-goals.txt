Future goals

Keep insertions with a codon-aware multiple alignment per lineage

Today every lineage member is placed on its germline's positions, counted from the junction,
and bases inserted relative to the germline are dropped (trees.R, reframe). That keeps one
germline per lineage and whole codons for IgPhyML, but a real somatic insertion shared by a
clade is thrown away, and it would have said something about the tree's shape.

The goal: align each lineage's members to each other, codon-aware, so a shared insertion
becomes its own columns. The tree builders would read the gap columns as missing data, and
IgPhyML would still see whole codons.

Open questions:
- Which aligner, and how to keep it codon-aware (insertions in whole codons, frame kept).
- How the germline is written where it has no base (a gapped germline per lineage).
- Which insertions are real: single-base ones are mostly sequencing or alignment errors
  (221 of 236 MiXCR insertions in one test set were one base long).
- Cost: one alignment per lineage, on donors with tens of thousands of lineages.

How often it matters, measured on 2,993 human heavy chains (Kim et al. 2022, donor 368-04):
IgBLAST reported an insertion in 35 rows (1.2%), Change-O in none (it drops them itself).
